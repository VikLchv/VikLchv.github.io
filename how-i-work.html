How I work

Six habits, each with a story

Anyone can write “team player” on a CV. Here is how I actually work, and the project that proves each point.

I work best with

  • a meaningful problem
  • clear context
  • real ownership
  • freedom in the how
  • fast feedback
  • capable, passionate people
Habits

What you can expect from me

I take ownership, even without a title

When nobody owns the outcome, I step in, find the facts and make it explainable.

Situation
A live reservation migration at HX. The data team was not invited to plan it, and the new system did not store when a booking was migrated.
Action
Rebuilt the migration status from the data, set rules the business could defend and hid duplicates through row-level security.
Result
98.5% of bookings deduplicated automatically, 30 mismatch scenarios, every booking auditable.
Reservation migration

I translate business into data

I start with the people who use the numbers, write down what they need and keep the decisions visible.

Situation
Four teams at Edwards wanted answers from the same sales platform. Later, DreamPro needed management reporting inside their own system.
Action
A requirements document and user stories for each team, an adoption plan per department. At DreamPro, a decision log kept with the client.
Result
One dashboard for four teams, and 39 logged decisions nobody has to remember.
Showpad analyticsDreamPro reporting

I turn ambiguity into a plan

Give me a vague need and I will find what matters, who is involved and a realistic first version.

Situation
CME wanted one view of TV viewership across the stations it owns in six markets, at minute level.
Action
Business analysis across markets, then a data model and aggregation layers designed for billions of rows.
Result
A model ready to centralise viewership data on Microsoft Fabric.
TV viewershipCapacity reporting, end to end

I teach what I know

A team gets stronger when knowledge does not live in one head.

Situation
At HX, I was the person holding most of the platform context.
Action
Trained colleagues on dbt, SQL, data modelling and Fabric capacity. Earlier I ran Power BI trainings, and today I write IT lessons for schools.
Result
More people who can own the platform, fewer questions waiting for one person.

I tell stories with data

A number only matters if someone understands it and acts on it.

Situation
Nobody at HX knew how much its guests contribute to science.
Action
On my own initiative I analysed iNaturalist data for the Head of Sustainability and told it through one humpback whale called Brigitte.
Result
HX makes up 47.2% of Greenland observations since 2024, 8× Lindblad, published as a LinkedIn story.

I work with AI, and I check it

AI is my diagnostic partner and my build team. The conclusion is always validated on the data.

Situation
Complex dbt models with grain and duplication issues, and a growing list of own projects.
Action
Used AI to locate issues in code and data instead of hand-writing long diagnostic queries, then proved the fix myself. Built my own agent system on Claude Code.
Result
Faster diagnosis without trusting a black box, and a personal OS that runs routine work.

Leadership

How I lead

Leadership is not how much I can carry. It is how much clarity and ownership I create around me.
  • I lead by building alongside people, not by directing from a distance.
  • I clarify the real problem and connect the people who need to talk.
  • I protect the quality of the outcome and raise the ambition.
  • I step forward when ownership is missing, then hand it to the right owner.
Working with me

A short manual

CommunicationDirect, honest and specific. I prefer a real disagreement to a vague yes.
FeedbackEarly and concrete: what happened, what impact it had, what would work better.
DecisionsDirectionally right and then iterate, rather than wait for perfect certainty.
LearningBy building. Enough theory to understand the model, then let me use it.
Ask me“What is your recommendation in one sentence?” It keeps me sharp.

Lessons

What eight years taught me

Technical problems are often ownership problems.Most broken numbers trace back to a decision nobody owned.
Historise everything that moves.History saved the migration. Missing history was the only thing we could not rebuild.
Responsibility needs authority.Without it, even a strong performer burns out. I now ask for both.
Validate, then trust.Every AI answer and every total gets checked on the data, row by row if needed.
